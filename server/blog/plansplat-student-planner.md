A biology lab report. A chapter to read. A history quiz on Monday. Somewhere in the mix, a permission slip needs a signature.

Students can know what is due and still struggle to decide what to work on next. A deadline tells you when to finish. It doesn't tell you where the work will fit between classes, the trip home, and everything else happening that afternoon.

I wanted another tool in [SplatWorks](https://splatworks.org/pages/splatworks.html) to help with that everyday problem. Meet **[PlanSplat](https://splatworks.org/plansplat/)**, a free student planner that runs in the browser.

If you like the quick capture of a to-do list, a weekly calendar, or a focus timer, you'll recognize the ingredients. I've put them together around courses, assignments, and time to study. You can start without an account, then connect Google when you want your planner saved in Drive and your deadlines sent to Calendar.

## What you can do with PlanSplat

Here's the feature tour before we get into the screenshots.

| Feature | What it helps a student do |
| --- | --- |
| Quick capture and detailed tasks | Record assignments, exams, projects, and personal reminders, then add priorities, notes, resource links, and checklists. |
| Courses | Keep work together by class, with teacher, room, color, and recurring class meeting times. Add a course while entering a task. |
| Today, Upcoming, and All tasks | See work planned for today, look ahead to deadlines, or find work that has no date yet. |
| Weekly study planning | See classes, deadlines, and reserved study blocks together. Keep a deadline separate from the day you intend to work. |
| Recurring tasks and routines | Repeat a task or check in on reading, reviewing notes, and other weekly routines. |
| Focus sessions | Choose one task, run an adjustable study/break timer, and keep a history of completed sessions. |
| Google connection | Load and automatically save a planner in Drive, and send deadlines and study blocks to a separate PlanSplat calendar. |
| Backups and exports | Download and reload a JSON backup, import/export task CSV files, export a calendar file, or print an agenda. |
| Recovery | Restore recently deleted items and choose between conflicting edits from different copies of a planner. |
| Language, color, and screen size | Switch among six language choices and four color themes; work on a phone, Chromebook, or desktop. |

## Start with what needs doing today

The **Today** screen brings overdue work and tasks due or planned today into one place. The quick-add box is there for the moment a teacher announces an assignment and the student needs to capture it before forgetting.

Type a task, add a due date if you know it, and choose **Add**. You can come back to fill in the details.

That matters to me. Capturing “finish the lab report” shouldn't require setting up the entire semester first.

The side panel shows upcoming deadlines, an estimate of today's workload, and the focus timer. Estimates are a planning aid, not a promise that every assignment will take exactly that long. If the day looks crowded, that is a useful conversation to have before the evening gets away from you.

## Give an assignment a few useful details

Open **Add task**, or select an existing task to edit it. An assignment can have a course, task type, due date and time, a day to work on it, estimated minutes, priority, and status.

[![Task editor with a biology assignment, separate due and work dates, priority, notes, a resource link and a three-step checklist.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/02-task-editor.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/02-task-editor.webp)

*One assignment can hold the directions and the next few steps, instead of scattering them across several reminders.*

I especially wanted the **checklist**. “Finish the lab report” can feel like one large job. “Review the observations,” “make the graph,” and “write the conclusion” give the student something specific to begin.

Notes hold the directions. A resource link can open a rubric, reading, shared document, or another SplatWorks tool. Task types distinguish assignments, exams, projects, and personal items. Priorities and the To do, In progress, and Completed statuses help sort out what needs attention.

## Add a course without losing your train of thought

Here's a small convenience that makes a difference. You are entering an assignment, realize its course isn't in the list, and don't want to abandon the task to set it up.

Choose **+ Add course** beside the Course field, or **+ New course…** inside the list. The course editor opens over the task. Save the course and it becomes the selected course on that task. The title, notes, and checklist you were entering stay in place.

[![Inline course editor for Art and Design with teacher, room, class times and Tuesday/Thursday meeting days.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/03-inline-course.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/03-inline-course.webp)

*The new course is created inside the task workflow. Cancelling takes you back to the task, too.*

Courses can include a short name, a color, a teacher or instructor, a room, meeting days, and start/end times. Those class meetings appear in the weekly planner. On a phone, use the **Courses** button above the task list to manage classes.

## Put study time on the week

This is the distinction I most wanted to keep: **a due date is different from a day to work on something**.

A paper due Monday may need attention Friday afternoon. A project due next week may need three shorter sessions rather than one long Sunday night.

[![Weekly planner combining biology, English and history classes with a Friday lab-report study block, a Saturday quiz-review block and upcoming deadlines.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/04-week.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/04-week.webp)

*Classes, study blocks, and deadlines share the weekly view, while keeping their different purposes.*

Choose **Plan time** on a task, or **Study time** in the week view. Set the day, start time, and number of minutes. You can connect the block to a task or leave it as independent study.

[![Study-block editor reserving 40 minutes at 4 p.m. to graph results and write a lab-report conclusion.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/05-study-block.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/05-study-block.webp)

*The student chooses the study time. PlanSplat does not automatically rearrange the day.*

The weekly view has previous/next week controls and a shortcut back to this week. On a narrow screen, the timetable scrolls horizontally.

## Look ahead and find what you need

**Upcoming** gathers future deadlines so a student can see the quiz or project before it becomes today's emergency.

[![Upcoming view grouping future English reading, a history quiz and a research project by deadline.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/06-upcoming.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/06-upcoming.webp)

**All tasks** also includes the work without a date. That's useful for “choose a science fair topic,” which deserves a place even before the due date is announced.

Search looks through task titles, notes, and course names. Course and type filters help narrow a long list. In this example, searching for “plant” finds the lab assignment through its notes.

[![Task search finding a biology lab assignment from the word plant in its notes.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/07-search.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/07-search.webp)

## Build routines without making them a competition

Some schoolwork repeats. For a task, choose daily, weekdays, weekly, or monthly repetition. Completing it creates the next occurrence, ready for the next round.

**Routines** serve a related purpose: choose the days for reading, packing a bag, or reviewing class notes, then record a check-in.

[![Routines view with reading, packing a school bag and reviewing notes, plus a checked reading day.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/08-routines.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/08-routines.webp)

*Routine check-ins are a private record. There are no public rankings or streak penalties.*

A teacher or parent could use this during a short planning conversation: What helped this week? Which routine needs a different time? The point is to support the student's next decision.

## Work on one thing for a little while

Select **Focus** on a task, then start the timer. The default is 25 minutes of study with a five-minute break, and both durations can be changed.

Pause when needed. Reset when you need to start again. The next study or break phase waits for you to start it.

[![Focus view with a selected lab-report task, a running study timer and demonstration entries in the recent focus-session history.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/09-focus.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/09-focus.webp)

*Completed focus sessions appear below the task list. The timer is connected to the work the student selected.*

The **Completed** view keeps a record of finished tasks. That can be useful when the student feels as if nothing got done, even after a busy week.

[![Completed view showing a finished biology observation log.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/10-completed.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/10-completed.webp)

## Connect Google once for Drive and Calendar

You can use the local planner first. When you want connected saving, choose **Continue with Google** and complete the Google connection.

[![PlanSplat connection window explaining Google Drive saving and optional updates to a separate PlanSplat Google Calendar.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/13-google-connection.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/13-google-connection.webp)

*This is the connection window before Google authorization. It does not show a signed-in account.*

The same connection requests access to files used with PlanSplat and calendars created by the app. After authorization, PlanSplat finds your planner in Drive, loads it, and automatically saves edits while the app is open and authorized. On another device, sign in with the same account to load that planner. A Google account is needed for these connected features, not for starting a device-only planner.

Calendar updates go into a separate **PlanSplat** calendar. Tasks need a **Due date** to appear there. A deadline without a time becomes an all-day event; a timed deadline gets a 15-minute marker. Scheduled study blocks use their actual duration. Class meetings and routines stay in the planner.

Changes flow from PlanSplat to Google Calendar. Editing an event in Google Calendar does not edit the task in PlanSplat. This isn't a Google Classroom assignment importer, either; students enter or import their tasks.

Google may require reconnection after a reload or when authorization expires. Sync runs while PlanSplat is open. Declining Calendar permission still allows Drive saving, and the header offers **Connect Calendar** or **Reconnect Calendar** when needed.

On a shared computer, use **Account → Sign out**, with the option to remove that account's device draft. Google Drive copies remain in Drive. Keep school account rules in mind; a school's Google Workspace settings may restrict connections.

## Keep a backup you can reload

I like having a file I can put somewhere safe. Google saving is useful, but a local backup gives you another way to keep your work.

Open **Backups & settings** and choose **Download planner backup**. PlanSplat saves a `PlanSplat.plansplat.json` file. Later, use **Import planner backup** to reload it on this device or another one.

[![Backups and settings with JSON download/import, task CSV import/export, calendar export, reminder controls and Google Calendar settings.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/11-backups-calendar.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/11-backups-calendar.webp)

*The JSON backup holds the planner's courses, tasks, routines, check-ins, study blocks, and focus history. It contains no Google authorization tokens.*

Importing a native backup **merges** it with current work. It doesn't wipe out the newer tasks you already entered. Keep backups before clearing browser data or changing computers.

Other ways to take your work with you include:

- **CSV task import/export:** useful for spreadsheets and compatible task exports. Review imported dates, course assignments, recurrence, and checklists; another service's CSV may not represent every feature the same way.
- **Calendar export:** download an `.ics` snapshot of deadlines and study blocks. Importing that file into a calendar does not turn on continuing sync.
- **Print agenda:** print the current task or weekly view, including through your browser's Save as PDF option.

[![Print layout of a PlanSplat task agenda with course labels, deadlines, notes and checklist steps.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/18-print-agenda.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/18-print-agenda.webp)

Browser reminders can be enabled while the app is open, and the focus timer can play a sound. Sleeping tabs and device settings can delay notifications. PlanSplat doesn't send push reminders after you close it.

## Recover a deletion or choose between edits

Accidentally deleted a task? Open **Recently deleted** in settings. Tasks, courses, routines, and study blocks can be restored there.

[![Recently deleted dialog offering to restore a practice task.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/12-restore.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/12-restore.webp)

If two copies contain conflicting edits, PlanSplat keeps the alternatives for review. Choose the version you want rather than having to guess which text disappeared.

[![Conflict review showing two different notes for the same demonstration lab assignment, each with a Keep this version button.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/19-conflict-choice.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/19-conflict-choice.webp)

*This example was made by merging two independently edited copies of the fictional planner.*

## Choose your language and colors

The language switcher offers English, Spanish, Vietnamese, Arabic, Chinese, and the shared Hindi/Urdu option, whose PlanSplat interface text is Hindi. Arabic uses a right-to-left layout.

Purple matches the other SplatWorks tools. If you prefer a different look, choose **Ocean**, **Forest**, or **High contrast**.

The phone layout keeps quick capture and task editing available, with navigation across the top and course management above the task list. The focus tools appear further down the page.

[![PlanSplat phone layout in English with task capture, filters and fictional coursework.](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/16-phone.webp)](/assets/plansplat-blog/v1.2.1/16-phone.webp)

Core local planning can also work offline after the app resources have been cached. Google saving and Calendar updates need an internet connection. Configuring Google sign-in on your own host requires an OAuth client for that address.

## Try a small school week

Start with one course and one assignment. Give the assignment a due date, then choose a separate day to work on it. Add two or three checklist steps and reserve a short study block.

That's enough to try the idea. A student has something to do, a place to keep the directions, and a little time set aside to begin.
