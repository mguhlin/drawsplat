    For District Reviewers

    SplatWorks NDPA / DPA Review Packet

    Current deployment scope

    SplatWorks is the ecosystem; DrawSplat is the whiteboard. The ecosystem includes the five office apps, standalone creative and productivity tools, classroom widgets, and games. ConceptSplat, MarkdownSplat, DiagramSplat, QuizSplat, RubricSplat, and SketchSplat VR are current tool names; former names remain search aliases and existing URLs and project formats remain valid.

    Match this document to the components and services actually approved for your installation. Apps Script console controls apply to the DrawSplat whiteboard’s Drive/Sheets workflows; they do not automatically administer standalone app drafts or district MariaDB/MySQL records. See compliance and district review and the component data and network inventory.

    Updated October 15, 2026. Branding changes do not amend an existing signed agreement or expand the components, services, or data processing approved by a district. Templates and review packets are not executed agreements or compliance certifications.

    Everything a K–12 district reviewer typically asks for in one place. The Student Data Privacy Consortium’s National Data Privacy Agreement (NDPA) is a standardized contracting framework whose standard clauses are not meant to be redlined — changes belong in exhibits. This page is the exhibit packet: product description, data elements by deployment mode, data flow, subprocessor list, security controls, breach SOP, retention, parent workflow, accessibility, and the FERPA / COPPA / SCOPE / CIPA crosswalks.

    District agreement status

    See the State & District DPA Register for executed agreements and pending reviews. This technical packet is not an executed agreement. Contracting parties, deployment scope, data categories, notice contacts, security obligations, and signatures must be confirmed for each district.

    1. Product description & approved educational purpose

    SplatWorks is the ecosystem of the DrawSplat whiteboard; WriteSplat, GridSplat, ListSplat, ShowSplat, and LayoutSplat office apps; standalone creative/productivity tools; widgets; and games. DrawSplat is a browser whiteboard with optional school-controlled Apps Script or district MariaDB/MySQL saving. Hosting the suite does not connect every component to that backend.

    Approved educational purposes may include whiteboarding, writing, spreadsheets, databases, slides, publications, diagrams, quizzes, rubrics, media creation, games, and classroom workflows. List only the components and external services the district actually selects in its agreement exhibits. See component features and current/former names. Optional AI or cloud services require their own approval; the SplatWorks operator does not use student content for advertising, profiling, or AI training.

    Which deployment is your district reviewing?

    Public website use: students visit splatworks.org or drawsplat.org for approved local apps, including the DrawSplat whiteboard. Editing stays local unless an enabled connection or sharing feature is used. The hosting/CDN services process request metadata such as IP address, user agent, and requested URL. Public website use involves data processing even without a student account.

    District-hosted installation with MySQL: the district hosts the application, API, and database and administers accounts, storage, access, backups, retention, and deletion. Student records and work saved through that backend are held in district-controlled infrastructure. Hosting or support access by any outside party must be documented; installing the software does not itself grant the DrawSplat operator access to the district database.

    Optional integrations change the scope: connecting a backend, enabling sign-in or cloud saving, sharing content, or opening third-party services requires a separate review of the selected feature and its data flow. The Google Apps Script option is also documented below but should be included in an agreement only if the district selects it.

    Before completing Exhibits A and B, confirm the district’s selected website or hosting location, enabled features, student data categories, and who can access that data. The district MariaDB/MySQL path supports private saving, roster-linked classrooms, assignments, student submissions, and feedback. Validate the actual deployment and its operational controls before student use; see Recommended production posture.

    2. Three deployment modes and what each collects

    ModeWhere data livesStudent identifiers collected
    Browser-only (public website or district-hosted static app, without a configured backend) The browser’s memory, localStorage, or IndexedDB, depending on the component; native project files and user-created exports. Projects may include text, records, quizzes, reports, imported originals, images, audio/video, fonts, or merge data selected by the user. Content may identify students. No student account required for local whiteboard or game use. Local content is not uploaded to a storage backend in this mode. The website host/CDN processes web request metadata; optional connected features require separate review.
    Google Apps Script (supported production) The teacher or district’s own Google Workspace tenant — Drive folders and a Sheet that the script creates inside the operator’s account. What a teacher chooses to enter: student name on a turn-in, class section name, age band the admin assigns. No password storage, no biometric data, no precise location, no advertising identifiers.
    District MariaDB / MySQL A MySQL database running on the district’s own infrastructure, fronted by a Node + Express API the district hosts. Same as the Apps Script row. Email + scrypt+pepper hashed password if the district opts into local email/password accounts. OAuth subjects if Google or Microsoft SSO is enabled. Optional Clever roster sync if the district connects it.

    4. Subprocessors

    Self-hosted local editing can run without an external storage or AI processor. Public hosting still uses a host/CDN: Cloudflare Pages serves the public sites and processes request metadata for the site operator. District-configured cloud, identity, AI, support, and hosting services require separate review. Record the actual contracting party; it is not always the district.TM as a product has no required subprocessors.     6. Breach notification SOP

    1. Discovery. A teacher, admin, parent, or external researcher reports a suspected breach via Contact / Information Request or the audit log surfaces an anomaly.
    2. Triage within 24 hours. Operator confirms scope: which deployment mode, which records, which audit trail rows are affected.
    3. Contain. Use the containment procedure appropriate to the backend: freeze supported Apps Script boards/rooms, revoke affected account sessions, restrict server access, or stop an affected service. Do not assume room-freeze controls cover district private boards.
    4. Notify within 72 hours. Operator notifies the named district Privacy / Security Contact from the District Data Privacy Addendum using the breach notice contact method declared there. Notice includes: what happened, what data was involved, what containment was done, what the district should do next.
    5. Investigate & remediate. Operator investigates root cause, patches, re-tests. The Activity Records audit log is preserved for the investigation.
    6. Post-incident report. Written summary delivered within 30 days, including timeline, scope, remediation, and any changes to security controls.

    8. Parent / student request workflow

    1. Parent visits Family Access Tools and submits a request (view, export, correct, delete, pause, safety report, privacy question).
    2. For the Apps Script workflow, the request lands in the ParentRequests sheet tab as pending_verification. District database requests follow the district’s separately published procedure.
    3. Compliance admin gets an email notification (Apps Script MailApp.sendEmail) and reviews the request in Teacher Admin.
    4. Identity verified via teacher-issued one-time 8-character code (SHA-256 hashed, 14-day expiry, single-use).
    5. Verified request transitions to verified, then approved / denied / completed by an admin click.
    6. Export: the Apps Script administrator downloads the student ZIP and delivers it through an approved district channel after verifying the requester. The console does not automatically email a temporary download link.
    7. Delete: admin button trashes Drive files, removes rows, logs DATA_DELETED.
    8. Acknowledged within 10 school days; completed within 30 calendar days.

    9. FERPA / COPPA / SCOPE / CIPA crosswalks

    FERPA school-official crosswalk

    When DrawSplatTM is engaged as a service provider, the district designates the vendor as a school official under FERPA’s school-official exception. Conditions:

    • Performs an institutional service or function. The product is operated as part of classroom instruction (the district’s approved educational purpose stated in section 1).
    • Under direct control of the school. The district controls the deployment mode (browser-only / Apps Script / MySQL), storage location, retention windows, and access rules; the District Data Privacy Addendum names the controlling district and the privacy contact.
    • Use / redisclosure limits. Student data is used only for the school-authorized educational purpose. No advertising, behavioural profiling, AI training, or sale of data. No redisclosure outside the district’s subprocessors.
    • Inspect / correct / delete rights. Parents and eligible students can submit a request via Family Access Tools; the District’s admin verifies and acts on the request from Teacher Admin.

    COPPA school-consent notice (for under-13 users)

    Under COPPA the district may provide consent on behalf of parents when the service is for an educational purpose and the district retains direct control. The district’s notice to parents typically says, in plain language:

    Our school uses DrawSplatTM, a free interactive whiteboard for classroom drawing, notes, and collaboration. The school controls all storage and access. DrawSplatTM does not show ads, does not sell student data, does not use student work to train AI, and does not require student accounts in browser-only use. If you would like to view, correct, export, or delete your child’s data, contact the school’s published privacy contact. Schools using the Apps Script family-request workflow may also provide Family Access Tools at /parents/.

    Texas SCOPE / TEC 32.151+

    See legal/texas-compliance.html for the plain-language explainer.

    CIPA

    CIPA is a school / library E-rate internet-safety and filtering obligation, not a vendor privacy law. DrawSplatTM supports CIPA via link allowlists, board freeze, text filtering, the moderation + image-approval queues, and the audit log, but does not replace the district’s network-level filter or its internet safety policy. Full statement: CIPA support statement.

    11. Recommended production posture

    Choose the installation that matches your district’s requirements: the complete district suite provides MariaDB/MySQL account-based saving, classrooms, assignments, submissions, and feedback; the optional Apps Script deployment provides Drive/Sheets workflows and its Compliance Console. Database installer and classroom checks have been exercised with MariaDB and MySQL. This does not establish a district-wide load rating or legal certification. Validate school Google sign-in, roles, backups, restoration, retention and record-request procedures on your own deployment before student rollout. See Compliance and district review.

    Ecosystem privacy controls and review

    Updated October 15, 2026. Your privacy controls provide origin-local inspection/export, explicit browser clearing, connection preferences, and optional 1-, 7-, or 30-day expiry. Existing drafts are preserved on upgrade; automatic expiry starts only after an explicit choice. These controls do not erase downloaded files, another origin, school servers, cloud accounts, or backups.

    Use the per-component inventory, retention schedule, provider register and notice process, and PROTECT deployment review to identify all approved components, ages, data locations and operational evidence. The five task tools keep API keys in tab memory and require approval before the first AI request. Student processing requires an approved API account with no-training/data-sharing settings and documented school/parent authorization; the checkbox does not establish a legal basis.

    Prospective policy deadlines are firm: acknowledge rights requests within 10 school days; fulfill within 30 calendar days; export/return and delete active stores within 30 calendar days of termination, and expire backups within 90 calendar days. A shorter applicable legal deadline takes precedence. Retain only a documented legal obligation with a stated end date, or irreversibly de-identified data with no re-identification. Restores must reapply erasures. Existing executed agreements remain governed by their signed terms and require agreement by the parties to amend.

    Parents and eligible students may request access, correction, export, erasure or pause through their school. Public-site privacy requests and registration for material provider/policy change notices: drawsplat@gmail.com. The public contact cannot retrieve private district records or browser data. Verify identity through established family channels and provide an assisted alternative. Schools must register a monitored notice contact. Material new student-data providers require 30 days’ advance notice and an objection, disablement or termination path; urgent security/legal exceptions require prompt notice and explanation.
