    SplatWorks GDPR Deployment Review

    Current deployment scope

    SplatWorks is the ecosystem; DrawSplat is the whiteboard. The ecosystem includes the five office apps, standalone creative and productivity tools, classroom widgets, and games. ConceptSplat, MarkdownSplat, DiagramSplat, QuizSplat, RubricSplat, and SketchSplat VR are current tool names; former names remain search aliases and existing URLs and project formats remain valid.

    Match this document to the components and services actually approved for your installation. Apps Script console controls apply to the DrawSplat whiteboard’s Drive/Sheets workflows; they do not automatically administer standalone app drafts or district MariaDB/MySQL records. See compliance and district review and the component data and network inventory.

    Updated October 15, 2026. Branding changes do not amend an existing signed agreement or expand the components, services, or data processing approved by a district. Templates and review packets are not executed agreements or compliance certifications.

    This page is for European and UK school administrators, IT leaders, and data protection officers (DPOs) deciding whether DrawSplatTM fits their compliance obligations under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) and the UK GDPR. It is not legal advice — your DPO should map the items below against your specific deployment.

    1. Who is the controller and who is the processor?

    Roles follow actual processing and contractual arrangements, not the ecosystem name or open-source license. A school determines the purposes of its educational processing. A self-hosted installation does not automatically give the source-code publisher access to district records.

    Public website hosting still processes request metadata. The site operator and hosting/CDN provider have responsibilities for that processing. School-configured Apps Script, cloud, AI, identity, hosting, and support services require their own role and contract assessment. Do not infer “no processor” or an exemption from contracting solely because editing is local. Review the ICO controller/processor contract guidance.

    2. Lawful basis for processing student data

    The school’s DPO should document the applicable lawful basis, national education requirements, approved purposes, data categories, and necessity for each selected component/service. Do not treat a browser permission, an app name, or a software license as legal consent. Where consent is relied on, document how it is obtained and withdrawn and how an alternative is provided where required.

    3. Data minimisation — what DrawSplatTM processes

    Collect only content needed for the approved activity. Users may enter identifying information in boards, office files, maps, quizzes, rubrics, media, or games even when no account is required. Imported originals, fonts/media, merge data, local drafts, and exported files may retain that information. Optional AI prompts and cloud saves add provider-side processing. The component inventory describes these boundaries; it is not a complete list of every browser key.

    4. Data subject rights — what the platform supports

    Use the component’s supported native save/export, edit, clear, and deletion controls, plus the deployment owner’s verified request process. Apps Script Family Access Tools and Compliance Console act on their configured whiteboard Drive/Sheets records; they do not locate every local app draft, export, MySQL record, cloud copy, or AI-provider request. District MariaDB/MySQL operations need their own tested procedures. Not every widget offers a JSON export. Identify all relevant copies and backups when responding to access, correction, restriction, portability, objection, or erasure requests.

    5. Data residency and international transfers

    Document where the site, logs, database, files, backups, and each enabled external service process data, including remote support access. Local-only content editing does not eliminate the hosting request metadata or optional provider flows. Assess applicable EU/UK transfer rules and safeguards for the actual services; this documentation does not certify a provider’s arrangements. See the ICO international transfer guidance.

    6. localStorage inventory (no cookies, no third-party trackers)

    Components use browser memory, localStorage and/or IndexedDB for settings, drafts, recovery, media, handoffs, and configured credentials. Records are specific to the origin and browser profile; splatworks.org and drawsplat.org have separate storage. Data can persist beyond a session until cleared. There is no suite-wide automatic expiration. Clearing browser data does not delete downloaded/exported files, synced copies, or backend records.

    Review the actual component and browser consent/storage requirements for your deployment. The presence of a shared consent banner is not proof that every application’s storage is covered or consent is legally sufficient. Do not put provider keys or identifying work on a shared profile without a clearing procedure.

    7. Security measures (Art. 32)

    Review HTTPS, authentication, authorization, least privilege, storage encryption, credential handling, backup/restore, patching, and incident logging for the selected configuration. Browser storage is protected by origin/device controls; it is not a promise of application-level encryption at rest. CSP and network origins vary by app. Bundled third-party libraries retain their notices; some selected features load external resources or call external APIs. Apps Script controls are backend-specific and must be tested before use.

    8. Children's data (Art. 8)

    Document approved ages, school/parent notices, supervision, the chosen lawful basis, and any consent/age-verification obligations that apply in the relevant jurisdiction. Do not assume a whiteboard age-band setting governs office apps, standalone games, or external AI services. Review service age restrictions before enabling connections for children.

    9. Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA, Art. 35)

    The DPO should determine whether the deployment requires a DPIA and complete the applicable assessment. Use the component inventory, NDPA/DPA packet, approved services, data-flow tests, security evidence, and district procedures as inputs. These public documents are not a completed DPIA or legal certification.

    10. Breach notification (Art. 33–34)

    Document responsible parties, contacts, escalation, investigation, notification, and evidence-preservation procedures for the actual deployment. The contractual response timelines in Terms and Privacy and the district addendum remain applicable within their scope; they do not replace statutory deadlines or a regulator’s notification requirements. Include hosting, backups, lost devices/exports, and external provider incidents.

    11. Records of Processing Activities (RoPA, Art. 30)

    Where required, record purposes, data subjects/categories, recipients, hosting locations and transfers, retention, security, and responsible contacts for each approved component and service. Include local drafts, imported content, exports, public-host logs, cloud saves and AI prompts where used. Retention configured in an Apps Script console does not govern unrelated copies.

    12. Honest gaps you should know about

    • No ecosystem-wide GDPR certification, completed DPIA, or automatic controller/processor agreement is supplied by these pages.
    • No single console discovers or deletes all browser, file, cloud, district database, and external-provider copies.
    • No blanket claim of zero network processing, no third-party code, or automatic 24-hour retention applies.
    • Contracts, transfer safeguards, accessibility review, age/consent procedures, security assessment and operational verification must match the chosen deployment.

    13. Quick checklist for your DPO

    1. List approved components, versions, origins, and optional services.
    2. Record data flows, roles, lawful basis, notices, contracts, and transfer safeguards.
    3. Verify local/device clearing, export handling, backend deletion, retention jobs and backups.
    4. Test access controls and rights-request procedures with representative teacher/student accounts.
    5. Document incidents, contacts, assessment results and remaining gaps before rollout.
