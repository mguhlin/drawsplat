Let students build quiz questions about morning and afternoon shadows so their explanations connect the observations to Earth's rotation.

## 1. Photographs of one shadow give students a change to explain

Same object, different time, different shadow. What happened between the photographs? That's a much more useful starting question than asking for a definition of rotation.

Gather photographs taken at several times, a globe or ball, and a stationary lamp. Spend eight minutes modeling a marked location rotating toward and away from the light. Give partners ten minutes to sequence the photographs and connect changing illumination to Earth's rotation. Keep the lamp in place. Discuss how the model simplifies size and distance; don't imply all outdoor shadows have identical directions and lengths.

*For planning: Grade 5 Science, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §112.7(b)(9)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch112a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. An answer key makes the explanation part of the quiz

Give pairs fifteen minutes in [SplatWorks QuizSplat](https://splatworks.org/solutions/quiz-flashcard-studio/) to create three questions: a straightforward observation, a misconception about the Sun's apparent motion, and a new example. Draft reasons for the answers on paper first.

Try the set with another pair or as printable flashcards. Ask players to explain what moves in the model, then choose one question or answer to improve. Save or export the set. Vocabulary cards and rehearsal at the globe can help a student find the words. With one device, use a projected set and paper explanations.

## 3. A drawn explanation checks the understanding behind the choices

Reserve seven minutes for each student to draw and explain a marked location moving from daylight to darkness and name one model limitation.

If the lamp starts circling the globe, you've found something to revisit. Bring the model back out and follow the marked location slowly. Give the student time to explain the change.
