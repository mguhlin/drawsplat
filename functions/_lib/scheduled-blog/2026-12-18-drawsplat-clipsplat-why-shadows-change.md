A short recorded explanation can help students show how a place on Earth moves from darkness into daylight while identifying what the classroom model simplifies.

## 1. Following one location keeps the model understandable

Where does morning begin for the little mark on our globe? Let students follow it slowly instead of trying to explain the whole planet at once.

Gather a globe or ball, a stationary lamp, and photographs of an outdoor shadow at several times. Spend eight minutes modeling the marked location rotating toward and away from the light. Partners get ten minutes to sequence the photographs and relate changing illumination to Earth's rotation. For today's clip, focus on moving into daylight. Keep the lamp stationary, discuss simplified size and distance, and avoid suggesting all shadows have the same direction or length.

*For planning: Grade 5 Science, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §112.7(b)(9)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch112a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. Watching the clip lets students check what their words and model show

Give pairs fifteen minutes with [SplatWorks ClipSplat](https://splatworks.org/solutions/clipsplat/). Frame the model rather than requiring a student's face. Rehearse what the viewer should notice, record or import a brief clip, and trim it to the essential explanation. Add a relevant title or image card.

Watch with a partner and choose one unclear connection to fix. Export MP4 and retain the notes. A teacher-provided clip is fine when recording is impractical. Students can practice the explanation aloud with vocabulary cards, or respond on paper beside one projected recording.

## 3. An individual drawing checks the idea beyond the shared clip

Use seven minutes for each student to explain a marked location moving from daylight into darkness and name a limitation of the model.

Can the student reverse the journey and still explain what rotates? That gives you a useful check on whether the model made sense, rather than whether the recorded lines were remembered.
