A station with equal-whole fraction models can help students explain why one-third is greater than one-sixth.

## 1. Equal-size wholes make the denominator comparison meaningful

Six is bigger than three. Does that make one-sixth bigger than one-third? Bring out the fraction strips before anyone settles the argument.

Spend eight minutes aligning equal-length strips partitioned into fourths and eighths, then compare one-fourth with one-eighth. Give partners ten minutes to extend the reasoning to equal-size wholes divided into thirds and sixths. Ask what the denominator tells us about part size when the numerator is one. Compare a shared-denominator pair as well, so students can explain when counting selected parts becomes useful.

*For planning: Grade 3 Math, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §111.5(b)(3)(H)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch111a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. A tested resource link gives partners a model to examine together

Allow fifteen minutes with [SplatWorks QRSplat Studio](https://splatworks.org/solutions/qrsplat/) to create a code for a teacher-selected public fraction resource approved for classroom use. Download PNG, put the full link beside it on a station card, and test it on a student device.

Students use the resource to draw or describe their model and justify a comparison symbol on paper. Have partners choose one unclear explanation to improve. Vocabulary cards and spoken rehearsal can help. With one device, project the model; the QR code is convenient access, not the evidence of learning.

## 3. New fractions check whether the comparison rests on the model

Reserve seven minutes for an individual comparison of new fractions sharing a numerator or denominator. Require equal-size wholes in the justification.

If the answer depends only on which written number is bigger, return to the parts together. Ask what each number means in the model. That's a conversation worth making time for.
