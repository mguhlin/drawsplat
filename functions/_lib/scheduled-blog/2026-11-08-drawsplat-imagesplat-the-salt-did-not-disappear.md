Let students explain where the salt went by weighing the complete system before and after it dissolves, then annotating a photograph of the evidence.

## 1. The balance gives students something their eyes cannot

Stir salt into water and you have a lovely little puzzle. Where did it go? "I can't see it" is a reasonable observation. Let's give students a way to test the conclusion that follows.

Spend eight minutes modeling how to weigh a covered container, water, and salt together before and after mixing. Keep every part in the measured system. Give partners ten minutes to compare the readings and describe what changed. If the numbers differ, look for spills, evaporation, or limits of the balance. No tasting, please; we're investigating mass.

*For planning: Grade 5 Science, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §112.7(b)(6)(C)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch112a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. A few useful annotations make the explanation easier to follow

Here's where [SplatWorks ImageSplat](https://splatworks.org/solutions/imagesplat/) comes in. Allow fifteen minutes to import a photograph of the investigation and label the before-and-after readings. Keep the source image on its own layer, then use arrows to show what was weighed.

Ask a partner, "Can you tell what stayed in the container?" Choose one confusing label to improve and check it again. We don't need a page full of arrows. We need enough information to explain why dissolved salt still contributes to the mass. Save a PNG and keep the original photo. Paper labels and an oral explanation work, too.

## 3. A different data set reveals whether the idea traveled

For the last seven minutes, give each student new solution measurements. Ask what the numbers support and what might explain a small difference. A vocabulary card or sentence beginning "The salt is still part of…" can help a hesitant writer get started.

I'd listen for a defined system and a sensible use of the readings. "The matter disappeared" tells you exactly where to pick up tomorrow.
