Put the author's words beside the reader's interpretation so students can explain the difference between a text's topic and its message.

## 1. Two texts about one park give purpose a practical meaning

"It's about a park." Fair enough, but what does the author want us to understand about it? That's the question I'd keep coming back to.

Bring a short park notice and an opinion about protecting the same park. In eight minutes, model finding a phrase that points to each author's purpose. Give partners ten minutes to compare the messages. The notice might tell visitors what they need to know; the opinion asks them to think or act. Have students show you the words behind their answer instead of stopping at "inform" or "persuade."

*For planning: Grade 4 ELA/Reading, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §110.6(b)(10)(A)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch110a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. Two board pages keep the source close to the explanation

Set aside fifteen minutes for [DrawSplat Whiteboard](https://drawsplat.org/app/whiteboard.html). Put the relevant wording on one page and the student's explanation on another. Text, shapes, and an imported image are enough. Connect a specific phrase to the message it supports.

Here's a useful partner job: ask, "Which words made you think that?" Let the writer choose one connection to clarify before saving the board or exporting a PNG or PDF. If devices are scarce, two sheets of paper do the same teaching job. Let students rehearse the explanation aloud before writing it.

## 3. A third text shows who can make the connection alone

Use the final seven minutes for a fresh, short passage. Each reader names the purpose, explains the message, and supplies a supporting detail.

If a student gives you the topic again, put their answer beside the author's wording. Ask what the author wants a reader to know, feel, or do. That small conversation may be more useful than another worksheet of purpose labels.
