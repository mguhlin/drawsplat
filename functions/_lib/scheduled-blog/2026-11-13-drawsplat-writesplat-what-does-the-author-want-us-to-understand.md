Give students two short texts about a school garden and ask them to explain how authors can share a topic while making different points.

## 1. A shared topic makes the difference in purpose easier to see

One writer describes the garden beds. Another asks for volunteers. Both are writing about the garden, but they want different things from us. That's a useful comparison for a young reader.

In eight minutes, model finding wording that reveals a specific purpose and message. Give partners ten minutes with the two texts. Ask them to choose a phrase from each and explain what the author wants readers to know, feel, or do. "They're both about gardening" gets the conversation started; the wording should take it further.

*For planning: Grade 4 ELA/Reading, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §110.6(b)(10)(A)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch110a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. A short document gives the explanation room to develop

Set aside fifteen minutes for [SplatWorks WriteSplat](https://splatworks.org/splatworks/writesplat/). Start with a claim, supporting details, and an explanation. Students can use their notes without copying whole passages into the document.

Have a partner underline the claim and identify its strongest supporting phrase. Does the explanation actually connect them? Let the writer choose a sentence to add, cut, or clarify, then save the document. Offer oral rehearsal or a simple sentence stem when a student knows what they mean but hasn't found the words yet. Paper is fine when one device has to serve the class.

## 3. An unfamiliar passage checks the reader rather than the document

Use seven minutes for each student to read a third short text and explain its purpose and message with one relevant detail.

If the response names only a purpose category, ask a follow-up: "What, specifically, is this author asking of the reader?" That tells you more about the next lesson than the appearance of the saved file.
