Keep the maps and accounts beside the writing so students can explain a railroad's effect on cattle transport without overextending the evidence.

## 1. Different sources let students check the transportation story

It can be tempting to see a rail line and fill in the rest: new markets, a growing town, jobs for everyone. Which parts do our sources actually establish?

Bring two dated Texas rail maps and short, reliable accounts of a town and cattle transport. Spend eight minutes modeling a cause-and-effect claim. Give partners ten minutes to locate a route and connect it to a change described in an account. Require a map observation and a written detail. Ask how a producer's experience could differ from a resident's, and leave room for towns that didn't share the same outcome.

*For planning: Grade 4 Social Studies, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §113.15(c)(4)(C)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch113a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. Separating evidence from explanation makes a gap easier to notice

Allow fifteen minutes with [SplatWorks MarkdownSplat](https://splatworks.org/solutions/markdown-studio/) under Question, Evidence, and Explanation headings. Students can draft from a heading scaffold, placing the map observation and account detail together before explaining the connection.

In the rendered preview, have a partner find the sentence linking rail access to the claimed effect. Choose one unsupported leap to revise. Export Markdown, HTML, or DOCX when the connection is clear. Paper notes, vocabulary cards, and a spoken rehearsal give students another route into the same writing task.

## 3. A second community checks whether the writer can use the method again

Reserve seven minutes for an individual explanation of one railroad effect in a fresh community account. Ask for the particular detail that supports it.

If a student retells the cotton or cattle example instead, return to the new source. What changed here? That's the history question we want the writing to answer.
