An annotated fraction model can help students explain why five-sixths is greater than two-sixths when both are parts of equal-size wholes.

## 1. Matching parts make the number selected useful evidence

Two pieces or five pieces? We can make a sensible comparison once we know the wholes and the pieces match. Let's make those conditions visible.

Provide equal-length fraction strips. Spend eight minutes aligning fourths and eighths to compare one-fourth and one-eighth. Give partners ten minutes to model a shared-denominator comparison and a shared-numerator comparison. Focus today's image on two-sixths and five-sixths. Ask why counting selected sixths works here, while a comparison such as two-fourths and two-eighths requires attention to part size.

*For planning: Grade 3 Math, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §111.5(b)(3)(H)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch111a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. Annotations show the reader what the comparison depends on

Allow fifteen minutes with [SplatWorks ImageSplat](https://splatworks.org/solutions/imagesplat/). Import a teacher-provided image of the fraction model, keeping the original on its own layer. Label the equal wholes, equal sixths, selected parts, and comparison symbol.

Have a partner identify which annotation explains why the symbol is correct. Choose one unclear label to improve and remove decoration that obscures the parts. Export PNG and retain the original. Students can describe the model aloud, use vocabulary cards, or annotate a printed version beside one projected example.

## 3. A changed pair of fractions checks whether the explanation still works

Reserve seven minutes for each student to compare new fractions sharing a numerator or denominator, using an equal-whole model to justify the answer.

If "five is bigger" is the whole explanation, ask five of what. A student who can answer that and show the matching wholes has given you something useful to build on.
