**Instructional Goal — Grade 3 Math:** Students will represent multiplication with equal groups and arrays and determine the total. This lesson addresses [Texas TEKS §111.5(b)(4)(D–E)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch111a.pdf). Students will demonstrate their understanding through a two-page explanation board and an individual response that explains their reasoning.

A student knows the product is twenty-four. Can they show where twenty-four comes from? An array gives the answer a structure students can explain and check.

I would keep this to a 40-minute lesson. The tool gives students a way to make their thinking visible, and the content goal tells us what that thinking needs to include. [DrawSplat Whiteboard](https://drawsplat.org/app/whiteboard.html) is the tool for today. Its [feature reference](https://splatworks.org/wiki/tools-widgets-features) describes the available options.

## Set Up and Model — 8 Minutes

Provide counters, grid paper, and a classroom problem involving equal rows of seats. Model three rows of four counters, connecting rows, counters in each row, repeated addition, and a multiplication expression. Explain which number represents which quantity rather than letting labels float free of the model.

## Investigate — 10 Minutes

Partners build four rows of six counters and connect the model to repeated addition and multiplication. Then rearrange the counters into six rows of four. Ask what changed and what stayed the same. Students should explain both the total and the relationship between the arrangements, using equal rows as evidence.

**Today’s focus:** Model four rows of six seats. Label the rows and the number in each row before naming the total.

## Use DrawSplat Whiteboard — 15 Minutes

Open the DrawSplat Whiteboard and build two pages: evidence first, explanation second. Use text, shapes, drawings, or an imported image to show the lesson content. Students should connect the claim on the second page to a specific detail on the first. A partner can question that connection before the student revises it. Save the board and export a PNG or PDF using the available options. Local files are enough for this lesson.

## Check the Learning — 7 Minutes

Ask students to represent a new five-by-three arrangement and explain how their multiplication expression matches its rows and columns. Collect this response independently, even if students created the earlier product together. Look for accurate content, relevant evidence, and a connection that makes sense. Use an incomplete explanation to choose a follow-up question or a brief reteaching example.

The [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles) connect through **S3.2**, communicating ideas clearly, and **S4.2**, setting goals and monitoring progress. Students set one improvement goal before creating their response, ask a partner to restate its meaning, and record what they changed. That gives you observable evidence for both indicators.

Offer vocabulary cards, an explanation stem, and oral rehearsal. With one device, project the tool and have students build responses on paper. Keep the same content target across options; the product should help students explain what they understand.
