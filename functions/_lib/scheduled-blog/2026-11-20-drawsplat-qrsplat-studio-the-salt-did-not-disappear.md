Give students evaporation evidence they can examine, then ask them to explain what an open container can and cannot tell us about matter.

## 1. An open container makes the measurement question unavoidable

Less water, a lower reading, and salt left behind. Where did the missing mass go? Let's make room for an explanation before declaring that anything vanished.

Spend eight minutes modeling a covered saltwater system with a balance: container, water, and salt all count. Follow classroom handling procedures and don't taste the solution. Give partners ten minutes with teacher-prepared evaporation data. Compare the measurements and ask what could leave an open container. Include spills and balance precision in the discussion. Keep dissolving separate from a chemical reaction.

*For planning: Grade 5 Science, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §112.7(b)(6)(C)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch112a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. A tested link gets partners to the same evidence

For fifteen minutes, use [SplatWorks QRSplat Studio](https://splatworks.org/solutions/qrsplat/) to make a code for a teacher-selected public resource approved for classroom use. Choose one that supports the evaporation discussion. Download the PNG, print the full link beside the code, and test it on a student device.

Students record a reading, a claim, and the explanation on paper. Have partners compare which detail they used and clarify one weak connection. The code is a convenient doorway; the response is what shows their thinking. With one device, project the resource. A vocabulary card or spoken rehearsal can help students explain the system boundary.

## 3. A new set of numbers checks whether students can explain the loss

In the last seven minutes, each student examines different before-and-after solution data and identifies a measurement limitation.

Ask what remained inside the measured system. If the explanation stops at "the mass went down," return to that boundary together. The number is the beginning of the discussion.
