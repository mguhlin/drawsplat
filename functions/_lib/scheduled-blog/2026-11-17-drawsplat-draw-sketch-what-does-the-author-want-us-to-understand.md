Have students sketch the connection between an author's message and specific wording in an animal description and a conservation passage.

## 1. Two ways of writing about animals make purpose easier to discuss

A factual description and a call to protect an animal might share plenty of vocabulary. That doesn't mean they have the same message.

Take eight minutes to model the distinction between topic, purpose, and message using two short teacher-selected passages. Give partners ten minutes to find wording that supports each author's intention. Ask them to explain what a reader is supposed to know, feel, or do. A label such as "persuade" needs a more specific explanation beside it.

*For planning: Grade 4 ELA/Reading, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §110.6(b)(10)(A)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch110a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. A simple sketch can show the connection without a page of text

Give students fifteen minutes with [SplatWorks Draw & Sketch](https://splatworks.org/solutions/drawsketch/) to draw a relationship between a source detail and the message it supports. Freehand marks, erasing, and PNG saving are enough for this job. Put labels in the drawing or on a separate sheet.

Before the artist explains, ask a partner what the sketch communicates. That can be revealing! Choose one mark or label to improve and try again. Save the PNG with a short written or oral explanation. Students shouldn't need polished drawing skills to show a sound reading of the text. Model one example on the projector if devices are scarce.

## 3. A new passage keeps the focus on reading

For the last seven minutes, have each student name the purpose and message of a third short text and support the answer with a detail.

If the drawing was clear but the new explanation is vague, return to the author's words together. A vocabulary card or oral rehearsal can help you tell a writing difficulty from an unfinished interpretation.
