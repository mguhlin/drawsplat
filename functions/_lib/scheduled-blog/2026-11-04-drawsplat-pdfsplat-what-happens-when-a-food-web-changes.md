**Instructional Goal — Grade 5 Science:** Students will predict how an ecosystem change affects matter cycling and energy flow in a food web. This lesson addresses [Texas TEKS §112.7(b)(12)(B)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch112a.pdf). Students will demonstrate their understanding through an annotated source packet and an individual response that explains their reasoning.

Remove one organism from a food web and students may say, Everything dies. That’s a prediction, but it needs some work. Ask them to trace a specific relationship and explain what might change.

For this 40-minute lesson, I would keep the product small. One accurate relationship and a supported explanation will give you more useful information than a crowded page. [SplatWorks PDFSplat](https://splatworks.org/solutions/pdfsplat/) is the tool for today. Its [feature reference](https://splatworks.org/wiki/tools-widgets-features) describes the available options.

## Set Up and Model — 8 Minutes

Provide a simple teacher-checked food web containing producers, consumers, and decomposers. Model arrows pointing from a food source toward the organism receiving energy. Explain that matter cycles through living and nonliving parts of the system while energy flows through it.

## Investigate — 10 Minutes

Partners trace two feeding relationships, then consider fewer grass plants after a dry period. They predict an effect on one consumer and explain a possible indirect effect. Students must distinguish a supported prediction from a certainty. Include decomposers in the discussion so the model does more than show who eats whom.

**Today’s focus:** Use a decomposer-decline scenario. Ask how slower breakdown could affect matter cycling, and distinguish that from energy flowing back to producers.

## Use PDFSplat — 15 Minutes

Open PDFSplat and load a teacher-provided PDF containing the lesson sources or model. Highlight a relevant detail, add a short explanation, and use drawing or text annotations to show the relationship. Keep highlights selective; coloring the entire page hides the evidence. Students can reorder pages so a reader encounters evidence before the conclusion. Export the annotated PDF and compare it with the original. Collect one explanation of why a chosen annotation matters.

## Check the Learning — 7 Minutes

Ask students to predict and explain one effect of a different organism decreasing, using an arrow pathway and a statement about energy or matter. Collect this response independently, even if students created the earlier product together. Look for accurate content, relevant evidence, and a connection that makes sense. Use an incomplete explanation to choose a follow-up question or a brief reteaching example.

The [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles) connect through **S3.2**, communicating ideas clearly, and **S4.2**, setting goals and monitoring progress. Students set one improvement goal before creating their response, ask a partner to restate its meaning, and record what they changed. That gives you observable evidence for both indicators.

Offer vocabulary cards, an explanation stem, and oral rehearsal. With one device, project the tool and have students build responses on paper. Keep the same content target across options; the product should help students explain what they understand.
