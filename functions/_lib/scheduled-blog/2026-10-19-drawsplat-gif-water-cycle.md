**Instructional Goal — Grade 5 Science:** Students will explain how energy from the Sun and water from the ocean interact in the water cycle and influence weather. They will create and explain a four-frame model addressing [Texas TEKS §112.7(b)(10)(A)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch112a.pdf). Success means connecting solar energy, evaporation, condensation, and precipitation in a causal explanation.

## Start with the Science

Set aside 40 minutes. Prepare an ocean photograph, a water-cycle reference, paper, markers, and a way to photograph drawings. Keep a digital drawing option available. Your students need enough time to reason before they start adjusting colors. You know how that goes!

**Notice and predict, 5 minutes:** Show the ocean photograph. Ask: “How could water here become rain over land?” Students sketch an initial explanation. Save it for the final comparison.

**Build the explanation, 8 minutes:** Use the reference to trace solar heating and evaporation from the ocean. Discuss cooling and condensation into tiny droplets or ice crystals, followed by precipitation. Clarify that water vapor is invisible; the visible cloud contains droplets or crystals. Connect changing atmospheric water to weather without suggesting every cloud immediately produces rain.

## Turn an Explanation into Frames

**Storyboard, 10 minutes:** Partners draft four frames: Sun warms ocean water; some liquid water becomes vapor; cooling supports condensation; precipitation returns water to Earth's surface. Add runoff returning toward the ocean to the final frame. Label energy transfers and changes of state. A cycle can begin at different points, so ask students to justify their chosen starting frame.

**Animate, 10 minutes:** Photograph or export the drawings as PNG or JPEG files. Import them into GIF Maker in the intended order. Set a readable delay, preview the loop, and download the GIF. Partners should explain the transition between frames before deciding the animation is finished. Assign a science checker and a frame organizer, then switch roles.

**Explain and assess, 7 minutes:** Show a partner the animation. Ask them to identify where solar energy enters the model and how the ocean supplies water. Revise a confusing arrow or label. Individually write three sentences explaining the Sun–ocean–weather connection, then compare them with the opening sketch.

## What to Look For

Assess the causal explanation, accurate phase changes, and a sensible return pathway. Beautiful animation does not compensate for water “disappearing” or clouds being labeled as vapor.

The [TCEA ELEs](https://mglearn.github.io/eles) appear in **S3.2**, communicating ideas through a model and explanation, and **S4.2**, monitoring progress by comparing initial and revised thinking. Offer picture vocabulary, oral rehearsal, and pre-drawn frames when drawing would become a barrier. A projected class animation works with one device.
