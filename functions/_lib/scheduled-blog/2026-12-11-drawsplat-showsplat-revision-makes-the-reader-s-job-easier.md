Let students explain how rearranging a paragraph improves its sequence, putting the original and revised wording where listeners can compare them.

## 1. A confusing sequence gives revision a problem worth solving

Every word may be spelled correctly, yet the reader still has to stop and sort things out. That's a good moment to talk about revision.

Prepare a short paragraph with a muddled sequence, a repeated sentence, and a missing explanation. Spend eight minutes moving one sentence and explaining how it helps the reader. Give partners ten minutes to propose two substantive changes and read the result aloud. For today's presentation, focus on the order of ideas. Keep the intended message; punctuation fixes and decorative adjectives don't solve a sequence problem.

*For planning: Grade 4 ELA/Reading, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §110.6(b)(11)(C)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch110a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. Three slides make the effect of the change available to listeners

Give pairs fifteen minutes with [SplatWorks ShowSplat](https://splatworks.org/splatworks/showsplat/). Use one slide for the reading problem, one for the original and revised evidence, and one for the explanation. Keep the text brief enough to compare and use speaker notes to rehearse.

Ask another pair to identify what became easier to follow. Choose a slide that needs clarification, improve it, and save or export PDF. Paper slides work, too. A student can explain the change aloud before writing it; vocabulary cards or a sentence stem can help name what the revision accomplished.

## 3. A fresh paragraph checks whether students can make a useful change alone

Use seven minutes for each student to revise one structural problem in another short paragraph and explain the benefit to its reader.

I'd ask, "What can I follow more easily now?" If the answer is only "it sounds better," read the two versions together and look for a more specific explanation.
