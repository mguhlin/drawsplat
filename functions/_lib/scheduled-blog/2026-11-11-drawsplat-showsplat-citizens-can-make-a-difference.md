Build a short presentation around a fictional neighborhood cleanup so students can explain how a voluntary civic action would actually work.

## 1. A cleanup gives civic participation a problem students can picture

"We should clean it up!" That's an encouraging start. Who can give permission? What can volunteers safely do? How would anyone know the effort helped?

Use eight minutes to model a request about a fictional public space. Provide a description of the responsible local office and enough details to plan an appropriate action. Partners get ten minutes to work out the permissions, volunteer roles, and community benefit. Keep this a classroom proposal; nobody has to contact an office. If the plan assumes children can alter public property on their own, that's a useful point to revisit.

*For planning: Grade 4 Social Studies, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §113.15(c)(15)(B)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch113a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. Three slides make the proposal easier for another group to examine

Try [SplatWorks ShowSplat](https://splatworks.org/splatworks/showsplat/) for fifteen minutes. Make one slide for the problem, one for the supporting details, and one for the proposed action. Short text and a relevant image beat a slide covered in paragraphs.

Partners can rehearse with speaker notes, then present to another pair. Ask the listeners to name the detail that makes the proposal workable. Choose one weak spot, improve it, and save the presentation or export a PDF. No extra devices? Sketch the slides on paper and give the same short presentation.

## 3. Another community problem checks the idea beyond the cleanup

In the last seven minutes, each student proposes a different voluntary action for a new fictional problem. Have them name who could help and explain the benefit.

Listen for an action someone could reasonably take, supported by a detail from the case. Vocabulary cards and a chance to rehearse aloud can help students share a good idea without getting stuck on the wording.
