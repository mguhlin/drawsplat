# DrawSplat and SplatWorks Minto editorial review

The 47 scheduled classroom posts now recommend a specific lesson in the opening sentence, give exactly three reasons, and place the teaching evidence beneath the reason it supports. The revised post files are the surviving drafts. Publication times, images and tags remain in place. The articles now live on SplatWorks.org, with redirects from their former MGuhlin.org URLs. Titles now lead with the classroom topic or question.

## Voice and title choices

The prose follows the conversational approach in [MockoFUN for Teachers](https://mguhlin.org/portfolio/articles/mockofun-for-teachers-a-free-design-tool/), [Walk and Talk Podcasts](https://mguhlin.org/portfolio/articles/walk-and-talk-podcasts/), and [Five Action Writing Strategies](https://mguhlin.org/portfolio/articles/five-action-writing-strategies-to-teach-critical-thinking-in-an-ai-world/): questions grounded in a practical problem, direct conversation with the teacher, contractions, and an invitation to try the idea. The posts retain one opening conclusion and three reasons. Each has its own examples and next teaching step, without adding personal experiences or classroom results the author has not supplied. The tool appears inside the lesson rather than in the title.

## Buried lede and three reason audit

Before the initial Minto rewrite, the original drafts stated a learning goal first, introduced a tool in the third opening paragraph, and only near the end said the product should help students explain their understanding. Their actual recommendation was implicit across those passages rather than stated in one sentence. Each entry below gives that sentence and the original source lines where its parts were hidden. Line numbers refer to the version before these revisions.

The four original headings were lesson phases, not reasons to teach the lesson. “Use the tool” and “make thinking visible” would overlap if treated as separate reasons; “export a PNG” is a task detail pretending to be a reason. The revised case has three distinct jobs: establish a worthwhile content problem, explain how the chosen medium supports its investigation or communication, and show how an independent response informs the next teaching decision. Together these cover the proposed lesson’s purpose, activity and assessment. They describe a teaching design rather than claiming measured classroom results.

## So what decisions

Each nonblank body line is accounted for below. Retained details tell a teacher what to teach, how to elicit evidence, how to help students express it, or how to assess it. Repeated tool-directory links, generic product promises and closing price statements do not carry one of those jobs and were cut. Safety, source limitations, paper access, standards and independent assessment survive where they affect the teaching decision.

The posts total 22966 words before revision and 14429 afterward, counting body text and Markdown headings. Each email below is under 150 words and is a draft for the author to use; none has been sent.
