**Instructional Goal — Grade 4 ELA/Reading:** Students will summarize a text while preserving its meaning and logical order. This lesson addresses [Texas TEKS §110.6(b)(7)(D)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch110a.pdf). Students will demonstrate their understanding through an annotated evidence image and an individual response that explains their reasoning.

A summary can be short and still miss the point. When students leave out the event that explains everything else, the remaining sentences sound tidy but tell a different story.

I would keep this to a 40-minute lesson. The tool gives students a way to make their thinking visible, and the content goal tells us what that thinking needs to include. [SplatWorks ImageSplat](https://splatworks.org/solutions/imagesplat/) is the tool for today. Its [feature reference](https://splatworks.org/wiki/tools-widgets-features) describes the available options.

## Set Up and Model — 8 Minutes

Choose a short text appropriate to today’s focus and prepare a deliberately flawed summary. Model selecting the central idea, necessary supporting details, and a logical order. Distinguish a summary from a collection of favorite details. Students should refer back to the text rather than rely on memory alone.

## Investigate — 10 Minutes

Partners select the ideas a reader needs to understand the text and organize them in a sensible order. They compare their choices with the flawed summary, finding an omission or distortion. Ask why a detail belongs. Their response should preserve the text’s meaning without adding a motive the author never supplied.

**Today’s focus:** Summarize a character’s problem and its resolution. Keep the turning point that explains the ending.

## Use ImageSplat — 15 Minutes

Open ImageSplat and import a teacher-provided image or photograph of the paper model. Add short text labels, arrows, and shapes that point directly to evidence. Keep the original image on its own layer so students can distinguish a source from their interpretation. Ask whether each annotation explains something a viewer could otherwise miss. Remove decorative marks that hide evidence. Export the annotated image as PNG and retain the source separately.

## Check the Learning — 7 Minutes

Have each student summarize a new short passage in three or four sentences, keeping its essential ideas and order. Collect this response independently, even if students created the earlier product together. Look for accurate content, relevant evidence, and a connection that makes sense. Use an incomplete explanation to choose a follow-up question or a brief reteaching example.

The [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles) connect through **S3.2**, communicating ideas clearly, and **S4.2**, setting goals and monitoring progress. Students set one improvement goal before creating their response, ask a partner to restate its meaning, and record what they changed. That gives you observable evidence for both indicators.

Offer vocabulary cards, an explanation stem, and oral rehearsal. With one device, project the tool and have students build responses on paper. Keep the same content target across options; the product should help students explain what they understand.
