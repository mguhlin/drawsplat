An illustrated caption can help students explain daylight turning to darkness when its accompanying model keeps the lamp fixed and Earth rotating.

## 1. A marked location makes rotation easier to follow

The Sun seems to move across the sky. In our classroom model, which object needs to move? That distinction deserves a little attention before the caption writing starts.

Gather a globe or ball, a stationary lamp, and photographs of one outdoor shadow at several times. Spend eight minutes modeling a marked location rotating toward and away from the light. Partners get ten minutes to sequence the photographs and connect changing illumination to Earth's rotation. Focus on the location leaving daylight. Discuss the model's simplified size and distance, and don't suggest every outdoor shadow has the same direction or length.

*For planning: Grade 5 Science, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §112.7(b)(9)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch112a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. An accurate caption makes students commit to an explanation

Allow fifteen minutes with [SplatWorks MemeSplat](https://splatworks.org/solutions/memesplat/). Pick a classroom template or local image, write a caption, and add two sentences explaining the model evidence behind it.

Have a partner check whether the image accidentally makes the Sun orbit Earth once a day. Choose one misleading phrase or mark to improve before downloading PNG. Humor is welcome when the science survives the joke. Students can rehearse aloud, use vocabulary cards, or draw and caption a paper version beside a projected example.

## 3. An individual model checks the meaning behind the caption

For seven minutes, each student draws and explains a marked location moving from daylight into darkness and names one model limitation.

Ask them to show what rotates. If that part is still unclear, revisit the globe and lamp together. A catchy caption is a useful invitation to explain, but the explanation needs to hold up.
