Put a fictional reservoir case in front of students so they can compare water-storage benefits with changes to downstream habitats.

## 1. A shared case gives different judgments something to stand on

More stored water may sound like an easy decision. What happens downstream? A useful comparison gives both questions room.

Prepare a fictional Texas reservoir case with a before-and-after map, a water-supply need, and three stakeholder statements. Label it as fictional. Spend eight minutes modeling a benefit and a cost, identifying who experiences each and which case detail supports it. Partners get ten minutes to compare water storage and downstream habitat changes. Ask what additional information would help. There's no required policy position; the explanations need to be fair to the case.

*For planning: Grade 4 Social Studies, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §113.15(c)(8)(A–C)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch113a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. Two board pages keep the consequences connected to their evidence

Give pairs fifteen minutes in [DrawSplat Whiteboard](https://drawsplat.org/app/whiteboard.html). Put the map and case evidence on one page, and their comparison on another. Text, shapes, drawings, or an imported image can do the job.

Ask a partner to locate the support for each claimed consequence. Choose one weak connection to clarify before saving the board or exporting PNG or PDF. Offer oral rehearsal and vocabulary cards so students can explain both sides. With one device, project the case and let students work on two paper pages.

## 3. Another environmental change checks whether students can compare fairly

Use the last seven minutes for an individual response to a new road-clearing case. Ask for the purpose, one benefit, and one cost, all tied to case details.

I'd look for a student who can explain a consequence even when it doesn't support their preferred choice. That gives you something substantive to discuss in the next lesson.
