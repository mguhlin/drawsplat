**Instructional Goal — Grade 3 Math:** Students will represent equivalent fractions with pictorial models and explain why the fractions represent the same portion of an equal-size whole. This lesson addresses [Texas TEKS §111.5(b)(3)(F–G)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch111a.pdf). Success means modeling one-half and two-fourths accurately and explaining their equivalence.

Two pieces must be more than one piece, right? That's a tempting conclusion when students count shaded sections without checking their size. A short video lets them show what happens when the wholes line up.

## Make the Wholes Match

For a 40-minute lesson, prepare equal-length paper strips, crayons, scissors, and a teacher-approved camera device. Provide a teacher-made sample clip if recording is impractical. Test importing and exporting on the devices students will use; available video formats depend on browser support.

**Launch, 5 minutes:** Display two identical strips. Fold one into halves and the other into fourths. Shade one half and two fourths. Ask students to predict whether the shaded lengths match, then align the ends. Follow with unequal-length strips to expose why “same size whole” matters.

**Build, 10 minutes:** Partners make their own models and label each fraction. Have them verify equal partitions before filming. One partner checks the wholes and partitions; the other explains the shaded portions. Switch roles with a second example, such as two-thirds and four-sixths.

## Make the Explanation Visible

**Plan and capture, 8 minutes:** Sketch three shots: show the whole strips, show each partitioned model, and align the shaded lengths. Students rehearse: “These fractions are equivalent because ___.” Film from above or photograph the three steps. Make sure the camera captures the strip ends; cropping them out hides crucial evidence.

**Edit, 10 minutes:** Import the clips or pictures into VideoSplat. Arrange them in order and trim anything that distracts from the comparison. Add a title naming the fractions and a spoken or written explanation. If adding captions, check every fraction name and symbol. Export the short video and watch it once to confirm the explanation remains readable.

**Assess, 7 minutes:** Show another pair the video. Ask the viewers to identify the evidence for equivalence. Then give each student a new pictorial pair to judge independently. Include one example with unequal wholes and ask why it cannot establish the same relationship. Score the model and explanation, rather than transitions or production polish.

## Keep the Goal in View

The [TCEA ELEs](https://mglearn.github.io/eles) connect through **S3.1**, effective teamwork with shared responsibilities, and **S3.2**, clear communication to another pair. Look for both students explaining the mathematics and viewers identifying the matching portions.

Offer pre-folded strips, fraction vocabulary cards, and an explanation stem. Students can create a paper sequence if recording or editing presents a barrier. A teacher can assemble those photographs into one class video afterward. The requirement stays the same: show and explain equivalent portions of equal wholes.
