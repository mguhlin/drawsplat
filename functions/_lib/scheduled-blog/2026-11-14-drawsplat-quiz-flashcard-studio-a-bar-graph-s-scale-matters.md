Have students write questions about a scaled graph, including answer explanations that show how they read the scale.

## 1. A small data set leaves time to explain the numbers

Let's keep the numbers manageable: fictional book-category totals of 6, 10, 8, and 4. The interesting part is how students read them from the graph.

Spend eight minutes turning the table into a labeled bar graph with intervals of two. Partners get ten minutes to check the bars, write a difference question, and find a combined total. Ask them to point to the scale before calculating. Change the display to intervals of one and ask which values stayed the same. Label the data as practice figures rather than a real class survey.

*For planning: Grade 3 Math, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §111.5(b)(8)(A–B)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch111a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. Writing the key makes students explain their own questions

Give pairs fifteen minutes in [SplatWorks QuizSplat](https://splatworks.org/solutions/quiz-flashcard-studio/) to make three questions: a direct reading, a likely scale mistake, and a new example. Draft an answer key with reasons on paper before entering the items.

Try the set with another pair or use printable flashcards. When someone picks an answer, ask how they got it. Choose one misleading question or weak explanation to improve, then save or export the set. Students can rehearse their reasons aloud or use vocabulary cards before writing. There's plenty to discuss even with one projected device.

## 3. A separate graph shows what each question writer has learned

For the final seven minutes, each student answers a difference question and a combined-total question on a fresh graph. Require an explanation of the scale.

A student who can write a clever distractor may still misread an unfamiliar axis. Use that individual response to decide who needs another example. The quiz-making is useful when it helps you hear that thinking.
