A simple diagram can help students explain why a pencil appears bent in water while keeping its actual shape separate from what they see.

## 1. A familiar pencil gives refraction a question students can test

Take a pencil out of the water. Still straight? Put it back. There's your invitation to ask what changed in the viewing, rather than in the pencil.

Use eight minutes to model a ray diagram and the meaning of its arrows. Prepare teacher-guided observations with aligned flashlight openings, a mirror, a pencil in water, and contrasting materials. Keep beams away from eyes. Partners spend ten minutes connecting observations to straight-line travel, reflection, refraction, and absorption. Give the pencil example particular attention: students need to explain light changing direction between materials.

*For planning: Grade 5 Science, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §112.7(b)(8)(C)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch112a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. A small drawing makes the proposed light path available for discussion

Allow fifteen minutes in [SplatWorks Draw & Sketch](https://splatworks.org/solutions/drawsketch/). Draw the pencil, water surface, and a simple light path. Add labels in the drawing or on a separate sheet. The sketch pad provides drawing, erasing, and PNG saving; that is plenty for this explanation.

Let a partner explain the drawing before its creator says anything. Choose one confusing mark to revise, then save the PNG with a short written or oral account. This is about the light, so artistic polish needn't decide the result. Vocabulary cards, oral rehearsal, and a paper sketch are useful alternatives.

## 3. An unfamiliar diagram checks whether students can explain the phenomenon

Use the last seven minutes for each student to identify and explain a new mirror or water-glass example.

If the student says the pencil really bent, compare the object in and out of the water again. The observation gives you a friendly way back into the explanation.
