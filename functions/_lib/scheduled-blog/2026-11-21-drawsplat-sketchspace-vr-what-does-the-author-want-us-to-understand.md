Help readers compare a museum information panel with an invitation to visit by connecting each message to the author's actual words.

## 1. A shared museum topic makes different intentions easier to spot

One text tells you about an exhibit. Another wants you to come through the door. They're related, of course, but what does each author want from the reader?

Spend eight minutes modeling a specific purpose and message, using wording from two short teacher-selected texts. Give partners ten minutes to find a supporting detail in each and compare them. Ask for more than a purpose label. "Visit this exhibit because…" says more about the invitation's message than "persuade" alone.

*For planning: Grade 4 ELA/Reading, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §110.6(b)(10)(A)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch110a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. Connected room panels give partners something to explain together

Allow fifteen minutes with [SplatWorks SketchSplat VR](https://splatworks.org/solutions/sketchspace-VR/) and its printable room template. Plan panels for the question, two text details, their relationship, a counterexample, and a conclusion. Keep the words readable; a wall of tiny writing won't become clearer in a virtual room.

Photograph the drawing, crop the panels, and preview in full screen or split view. Glasses are optional. Ask a partner to explain the connection across two panels, choose one panel to clarify, and download the face images. Students can rehearse aloud first. A paper room and a projected preview are enough when devices are limited.

## 3. A short independent reading checks the meaning behind the room

Use seven minutes for a third text. Each student identifies the purpose, explains the message, and points to a relevant phrase.

If the response slides back to "it's about a museum," ask what the reader is supposed to know or do. That's the distinction worth carrying into the next lesson, wherever students display their work.
