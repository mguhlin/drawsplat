A globe with two marked locations can help students explain how one place on Earth can face the Sun while another faces away.

## 1. Two locations make the day-and-night relationship visible

It's daylight here. Does that mean it's daylight everywhere? Put two marks on a globe and let students investigate the question.

Use a globe or ball, a stationary lamp, and photographs of one outdoor shadow at several times. Spend eight minutes modeling rotation with the two marked locations. Give partners ten minutes to follow which mark faces the lamp and relate changing illumination to Earth's rotation. Keep the lamp in place. Discuss the model's simplified size and distance, and avoid treating all outdoor shadows as identical in direction or length.

*For planning: Grade 5 Science, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §112.7(b)(9)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch112a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. Labeled map links ask students to explain the relationship in words

Allow fifteen minutes in [SplatWorks ConceptSplat](https://splatworks.org/solutions/concept-map/). Add nodes for Earth's rotation, the two locations, sunlight, and darkness. Label each link so it can be read as a sentence, with notes identifying the model observations.

Ask a partner to follow a location through the map. Choose one vague connection to clarify, then save editable JSON and export PNG. Students can speak the sentences first or use vocabulary cards. Paper nodes beside a projected model work when devices are limited; the labels still need to explain what is happening.

## 3. One location's journey checks whether the relationship is understood

Give each student seven minutes to sketch one marked place turning away from the light, explain the change, and identify something the model simplifies.

If the student can label the two sides but cannot explain the change, rotate the globe slowly together. Ask what the mark is moving toward or away from. That brings the labels back to an observable relationship.
