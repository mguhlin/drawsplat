**Instructional Goal — Grade 4 ELA/Reading:** Students will support an inference about a character with relevant text evidence and explain how that evidence supports their thinking. The response addresses [Texas TEKS §110.6(b)(7)(C)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch110a.pdf). Success means stating a reasonable inference, locating a supporting passage, and explaining the connection.

“He's brave.” That's a start. But where did the reader get that idea? A recording gives students a chance to hear their own explanation. Sometimes the missing evidence becomes obvious as soon as they press Play.

[DrawSplat AudioSplat](https://splatworks.org/solutions/audiosplat/) records microphone audio, imports files, and lets students arrange and edit tracks. For this 35-minute lesson, I would skip the music and sound effects. A short spoken response gives you plenty to work with.

## Give Students Something Worth Discussing

Choose a brief story your class has already read, with a character whose actions support more than one reasonable interpretation. Have paper copies with paragraph numbers, headphones, and a teacher-approved recording device ready. Keep recordings in the classroom workflow; students do not need a public audience for this task.

**Model, 5 minutes:** Offer a claim about the character and read a supporting passage. Think aloud: “This action makes me think ___ because ___.” Then give a weak example that names a trait but never connects it to the story. Ask students what a listener would still need to know.

**Read and plan, 8 minutes:** Students reread independently and choose one inference. They mark a relevant passage and jot three prompts: *my idea, the evidence, how it connects*. Partners challenge an unsupported interpretation by asking, “Which words helped you decide?” Accept different inferences when students can defend them.

## Record, Listen, Revise

**First recording, 7 minutes:** Open AudioSplat and check microphone permission. Record a 30–45-second response. Students identify the paragraph, quote a short phrase or paraphrase accurately, and explain its significance. They can rehearse before recording. Download the audio locally using an available export format; WAV is a straightforward option.

**Listen and improve, 8 minutes:** Partners listen once without interruption. The listener names the claim and the evidence they heard. If either is unclear, the speaker revises notes and records again. Keep the first version so students can compare their reasoning. Trimming a long silence is useful; polishing every breath isn't the goal.

**Individual check, 7 minutes:** Students write a three-sentence response to a second question about the same character. Compare this with the recording. Assess the inference, relevance of evidence, and explanation. A confident voice alone doesn't show reading comprehension.

## Connect the Learning

The [TCEA ELEs](https://mglearn.github.io/eles) fit through **S3.2**, communicating ideas clearly, and **S4.2**, monitoring progress. The evidence is the listener's accurate restatement and the student's documented improvement between recordings.

Offer oral rehearsal in a home language, sentence stems, and a partner who can help locate paragraphs. A student may dictate, use supported communication, or submit a written explanation. With one device, run a recording station while classmates plan on paper. Keep the reading target consistent across options.
