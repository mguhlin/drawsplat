Connect a flashlight investigation to a drawing of the light's path so students can explain straight-line light travel.

## 1. A blocked opening gives the ray diagram something to explain

Line up the openings in several cards and shine a flashlight through them. Move one card. What happened? That small change gives students a reason to draw arrows with care.

Use eight minutes to model a ray diagram and explain what the arrows represent. Prepare teacher-guided stations for aligned openings, a mirror, a pencil in water, and contrasting light and dark materials. Follow light safety procedures; keep beams away from eyes. Partners get ten minutes to connect observations to straight-line travel, reflection, refraction, and absorption. For today's board, focus on the aligned openings and the blocked path.

*For planning: Grade 5 Science, 40 minutes; [Texas TEKS §112.7(b)(8)(C)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch112a.pdf). [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles): S3.2 and S4.2.*

## 2. Two pages connect the observation to the explanation

Give students fifteen minutes in [DrawSplat Whiteboard](https://drawsplat.org/app/whiteboard.html). Place a drawing or teacher-provided image of the investigation on one page. On the second, explain the light path and connect it to a detail on the first.

Ask a partner to follow the arrows. Do they show the light's travel, or just point at objects? Choose one unclear mark to improve, then save the board or export PNG or PDF. A vocabulary card and spoken explanation can precede the writing. Two paper pages work when a projected device is all you have.

## 3. A different diagram checks the idea beyond the flashlight

Reserve seven minutes for an individual response to a new mirror or water-glass diagram. Ask students to name the phenomenon and explain what the light is doing.

If the answer is only a label, follow up with "Show me the path." That can tell you whether to revisit the observation or the drawing convention.
