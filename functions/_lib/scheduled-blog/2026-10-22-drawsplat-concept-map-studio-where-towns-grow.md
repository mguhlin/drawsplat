**Instructional Goal — Grade 4 Social Studies:** Students will explain how landforms and climate influence settlement and identify settlement patterns at different times. This lesson addresses [Texas TEKS §113.15(c)(7)(A–B)](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/sboe-rules-tac/sboe-tac-currently-effect/ch113a.pdf). Students will demonstrate their understanding through a labeled relationship map and an individual response that explains their reasoning.

A river on a map is easy to spot. Explaining why a town grew beside it takes another step. I would give students two dated Texas maps and ask what changed, then let them build an explanation they can defend.

I would keep this to a 40-minute lesson. The tool gives students a way to make their thinking visible, and the content goal tells us what that thinking needs to include. [SplatWorks ConceptSplat](https://splatworks.org/solutions/concept-map/) is the tool for today. Its [feature reference](https://splatworks.org/wiki/tools-widgets-features) describes the available options.

## Set Up and Model — 8 Minutes

Use teacher-selected historic and modern maps of the same Texas area, a short account of transportation, water access, and climate, and a blank comparison sheet. Model one claim about settlement, identifying a map feature and a detail from the account. Distinguish an observation from a possible explanation; a map alone cannot establish every cause.

## Investigate — 10 Minutes

Partners mark settlements on both maps, identify a pattern, and connect it to water, terrain, or climate. Ask them to consider another influence, such as transportation or employment. Their response should show two map details and explain a geographic connection without claiming geography was the only cause. Give them this question: Which evidence would make you revise your explanation?

**Today’s focus:** Compare settlements beside a river with settlements farther inland. Ask which map details support water access as one explanation and what evidence is still missing.

## Use ConceptSplat — 15 Minutes

Open ConceptSplat and start with a blank map or a suitable organizer. Add one node for each important idea, then connect the nodes with labels that explain the relationship. Students should read each connection as a sentence. A line without a meaningful label is a cue to revisit the explanation. Add a source note where evidence enters the map. Save the editable JSON and export a PNG for discussion.

## Check the Learning — 7 Minutes

Give students a third small map and ask them to suggest a plausible settlement location, explain one geographic advantage, and name an uncertainty. Collect this response independently, even if students created the earlier product together. Look for accurate content, relevant evidence, and a connection that makes sense. Use an incomplete explanation to choose a follow-up question or a brief reteaching example.

The [TCEA Essential Learning Expectations](https://mglearn.github.io/eles) connect through **S3.2**, communicating ideas clearly, and **S4.2**, setting goals and monitoring progress. Students set one improvement goal before creating their response, ask a partner to restate its meaning, and record what they changed. That gives you observable evidence for both indicators.

Offer vocabulary cards, an explanation stem, and oral rehearsal. With one device, project the tool and have students build responses on paper. Keep the same content target across options; the product should help students explain what they understand.
